# From a request to a usable plan

Read this for a complete itinerary, an existing-plan review or a revision with downstream effects.
Use only the stages needed for the request. See the [worked example](../examples/hokuriku-six-days.md)
when a concrete planning/review/handoff example would help; its dated facts are not live results.

## Establish the working plan

Extract a compact brief from what the user has supplied: dates and endpoints, people and transport,
interests/pace, budget where relevant, must-visits, fixed commitments and flexible choices. Ask only
about missing information that materially changes feasibility; otherwise state useful assumptions.
Do not require a form or force the user to approve an outline before completing a requested plan.

Start with city order, overnight bases and daily areas. Explain the important trade-offs: fewer hotel
moves, luggage handling, daylight or admission windows, and travel versus visit time. Separate day
trips from overnight bases. Keep attractive alternatives outside the main schedule unless there is
room for them; a candidate list is not a day plan.

Maintain one working plan with dates, bases, daily priorities, key movement legs, venue choices and
open decisions. A short table is sufficient; stable IDs help when several views or revisions are
involved. Keep plan role (fixed/selected/candidate/backup) separate from evidence status. Treat
user-reported reservations as constraints, without accessing or claiming to verify their accounts.

## Research the decisions that matter

Prioritize claims whose failure would break the plan: fixed arrival/departure deadlines, admission
windows, required connections, eligibility and meal conditions. Then refine the choices requested by
the user. Do not query every tool or every alternative as a completeness ritual.

For a timed chain, work forward from actual readiness, including visits, walking, luggage and
transfer allowances, and backward from fixed deadlines. A later train is not a fix unless the
destination's remaining visit window still works. Separate published durations from agent estimates.
Keep the query's date, place, seat/meal conditions and original link with its result.

Include local access to the actual visit, not just travel between city stations. Resolve local
connections when a tight opening window or infrequent service makes them consequential; broad
plans can retain labelled estimates. If a selected connection leaves a substantial idle wait,
consider whether another supported connection or more time at the visit better fits the brief.
Do not minimize every wait at the expense of transfer margin or query every departure.

Give a useful day structure with priorities, movement and recovery time. Make a requested rainy-day
alternative actionable on that date; an indoor venue with unchecked closure conditions is only a
candidate, and resting or shortening the day can be a valid fallback. Match the research depth to
the user's requested precision. Missing facts stay explicit rather than blocking unrelated work.

## Review and repair

Extract the existing plan before judging it. Retain fixed dates, accommodation and must-visits;
explain incompatible commitments instead of silently replacing one. Do not label a preference for
a busy or quiet day as an objective error.

| Finding | What to show |
| --- | --- |
| Confirmed conflict | The affected day/leg, applicable evidence, consequence and smallest workable repair |
| Recommendation | The estimated pace/routing issue, assumptions, trade-off and optional adjustment |
| Unresolved check | The exact missing fact, its impact and the decision that depends on it |

Implement the repair in the requested output, not only in a warning beside an unchanged schedule.
If it cannot be resolved, identify the blocked leg/choice. Recheck affected connections and visit
conditions; keep still-applicable evidence for unchanged parts. Do not query a failed source again
to complete a checklist or imply that a different source verified its inaccessible page.

For a local change, record what moved and propagate it to the daily plan, key legs, meal conditions,
open decisions and any requested presentation/map. Mark an older view as superseded if it cannot
be updated. Moving an uncertain venue does not make it verified.

Treat a conditional fallback as a complete branch: state its trigger, changed visits and onward
movement, and any affected cost or deadline. Do not attach a main-plan return service to a fallback
that finishes after it departs. Where queues or unavailable luggage storage would materially change
the day, give a decision point and a feasible alternative; do not promise an alternative has no wait.

## Handoff and completion

Lead with the usable result. A full plan usually needs a date/base overview, daily priorities and
movements, key dated connections, requested hotel/meal choices and the remaining decisions. A review
usually needs findings and the corrected affected days; do not rewrite unaffected days unnecessarily.
Use [the deliverable guide](deliverables.md) only for requested slides, maps or coordinated files.
Plain text/Markdown can be the complete deliverable.

Before finishing, reconcile dates and nights, forward connections, fixed commitments and source
conditions. Check that the output actually contains the requested choices and original links, that
backups fit their proposed dates, and that optional stops are not counted as mandatory visits.
Describe the verified scope: selected checks with no detected conflict do not certify the whole trip.
Leave the next user decisions specific, such as choosing between two hotel candidates or checking
a particular luggage arrangement. Execute no reservation, ticket purchase or account workflow.
